A cinematic emotional track written to an absent father, built from unanswered questions, silence, grief, contradiction, and the damage left behind when a child has to grow around a hole.

Background

Answer Me This was made from the questions a child never got to ask, carried into adulthood by a man who still feels the weight of the silence.

The song is written to an absent father — not as a clean accusation, but as a confrontation with the empty space he left behind. It comes from growing up with fragments instead of answers: small memories that almost feel warm, stories that made him sound dangerous, moments where he seemed safe, and then years of nothing. No goodbye. No explanation. No real truth to build from.

That is what makes the track so painful. The father is not a full person in the memory. He is a shadow with blood attached to it. Part threat, part protector, part stranger, part ghost. A child was left trying to understand how the same man could feel scary, safe, absent, and important all at once.

Answer Me This is built around that contradiction. It asks whether he left by choice or got pulled under by life. Whether he thought about his children. Whether he tried to find them. Whether he knew what happened after he was gone. Whether the silence was cruelty, weakness, shame, or something more complicated. The song does not pretend one answer would fix everything, but it makes clear that not having answers became its own kind of damage.

Under the anger, there is grief. Under the pride, there is still a son who wanted to know if he mattered. That is the hardest part of the song: the voice says it does not need him, but the body tells the truth. The throat tightens. The chest reacts. The old wound still knows exactly where it lives.

The instrumental should carry that unresolved feeling. Heavy, cinematic, and emotional, but not overdramatic. It needs space for the words to hit, because the power is in the questions. The sound should feel like walking through an old house full of memories that do not fully belong to you anymore — dark, tense, wounded, but controlled enough to show that this is not a breakdown. It is a reckoning.

At its core, Answer Me This is not a request for a repaired bond.

It is not begging for a father to return.

It is the demand for the truth that should have been given to the child before he had to build himself around the hole.

And if the father ever hears it, the message is simple:

Now you know what your silence left behind.
