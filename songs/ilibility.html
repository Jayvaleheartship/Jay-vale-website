Background

Ilibility was made from the frustration of being explained by people who sound intelligent but still fail to actually read the person in front of them.

The track takes aim at the kind of clinical language that can make a person feel more reduced than understood. Words like irritability, lability, rigidity, vulnerability, reactivity, grandiosity — they all sound official, polished, and important. But when they are thrown around without real human connection, they start to feel less like insight and more like a performance of authority.

At the center of the song is the fake word Ilibility: a sarcastic invention for the fake ability to label someone with intelligent-sounding words while missing the actual human underneath. It turns the language back on the room. If professionals can dress assumptions in complicated terms, then the song can dress its anger in the same rhythm and expose how ridiculous it sounds when the humanity disappears.

The anger in Ilibility is not about rejecting every form of help. It is about rejecting being talked down to. It is about the moment where “therapy language” stops feeling like care and starts feeling like hierarchy. The person in the chair is not refusing to be understood — they are refusing to be simplified, boxed in, or treated like a checklist with a pulse.

The instrumental was built to support that sarcastic attack: sharp, playful, slightly arrogant, and confrontational. It needs bounce because the track is funny, but it also needs bite because the joke comes from something real. The rhythm of the repeated “-ility” words turns the jargon itself into percussion, making the clinical language sound mechanical, smug, and almost absurd.

At its core, Ilibility is a protest against being diagnosed by delivery instead of understood through listening.

It is not saying, “Do not analyze me.”

It is saying:

Talk to me like a human.

Drop the performance.

Lose the fake superiority.

And stop confusing complicated words with actual understanding.
