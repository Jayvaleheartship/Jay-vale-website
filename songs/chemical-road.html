Background

Chemical Roads was made from the feeling of driving through your own head and not knowing which part of you is behind the wheel.

The song came from the idea that a mind can become a road system: some roads are familiar, some are damaged, some keep leading back to places you swore you would never visit again. The “chemicals” are not just about science or the brain in a clean textbook way. They are about mood, memory, survival, panic, numbness, adrenaline, hope, anger, and the strange moments where everything inside you changes without asking permission.

It was written from the perspective of someone who keeps moving because stopping is dangerous. The road becomes a metaphor for coping: driving forward, making music, distracting yourself, chasing noise, chasing meaning, trying not to turn inward too deeply. Every thought feels like a turn. Every emotion feels like weather. Some days the chemicals lift you just enough to see light in the distance. Other days they drag the whole sky down.

Musically, Chemical Roads belongs in the Jay Vale world: dark, cinematic, emotional, and direct. It was built to feel like headlights cutting through fog. The sound carries the weight of late-night thoughts, heavy drums, haunting melodies, and vocals that sit somewhere between confession and confrontation. It is not a clean victory song. It is a survival track. The kind of song that does not pretend the road is safe, but still refuses to park the car and disappear.

At its core, Chemical Roads is about the invisible fight inside the body and mind. The battle between wanting peace and being wired for war. Between knowing what is happening and still not being able to stop it. It is about trying to understand yourself when your own chemistry keeps changing the map.

The song was made for anyone who has ever felt like their emotions were not a choice, but a road they were forced to walk. Or drive. Or crawl through.

And still, somehow, keep going.
